\textit{Proof}.

\textsc{Base Case}
$P(1)$ from the definition of an empty collection of primes $n=1$

Hence $P(1)$ holds

\textsc{Inductive Case}
Assume $P(k)$ holds for $k > 1$

we will now show $P(k+1)$

\textsc{Case I} $k+1$ is a prime therefore the ordered collection of primes involved is $\{k+1\}$

\textsc{Case II} $k+1$ is a composite which means

$k+1 = a \cdot b$ Since $a \leq k$ and $b \leq k$ that means $P(a), P(b)$ both holds

$a$ has an ordered collections of primes $Q$ and $b$ has an ordered collections of primes $R$ as well

which means $k+1$ has $Q + R$ ordered collection of primes therefore $P(k+1)$ holds

by weak induction $P(n)$ holds

\qed
