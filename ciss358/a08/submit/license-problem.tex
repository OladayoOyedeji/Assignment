\textsc{[Greedy]}
\\
You are starting a consulting company that needs to obtain licenses for
$n$ different pieces of software. Due to regulations, you can only
obtain these licenses at the rate of at most one per month.

Each license is currently selling for a price of \$100. However, they
become
 more expensive according to exponential growth curves: in
particular, the cost of license $i$ increases by a factor of $r_i > 1$ each month, where
$r_i$ is a given parameter. 
This means that if license $i$ 
is purchased $t$ months from
now, it will cost $100 \cdot r_i^t$. 
We will assume that all the price growth rates are
distinct; that is, $r_i \neq r_j$ for licenses 
$i \neq j$ (even though they start at the same
price of \$100).

Given that you can only buy at most one license
a month, in which order should you buy the licenses so that the total amount of
money you spends is as small as possible?

Describe an algorithm that takes the $n$ rates of price growth $r_0, r_1, \ldots, r_{n-1}$, and
computes an order in which to buy the licenses so that the total amount of
money spent is minimized.
State the runtime of your algorithm.
The running time of your algorithm should be
polynomial in $n$.

For this problem, you only need to describe your algorithm.
Make sure you describe your algorithm clearly.
